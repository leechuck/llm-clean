# Dependence (D)

A property is **externally dependent** when nothing can be an instance of it all by itself: being that kind of thing necessarily requires some **other, separate** entity to exist.

Three qualifications:

1. **Separate.** The required entity must lie **outside** the instance — not a part, not the matter it is constituted of, not one of its members. Everything needs its own parts; that is not what is measured here.
2. **Generic.** *Some* instance of the other class must exist, not one particular individual. A student who transfers schools is still dependent.
3. **Definitional, not causal.** Background conditions — air, gravity, sunlight, spacetime, a creator — do not count. The other entity must appear in the class's *definition*, not merely among the preconditions of its existence.

D has only two values; there is no `~D`.

**`+D`** — every instance necessarily coexists with some separate entity of another kind: *Student* (some educational institution), *Employee*, *Parent*, *Spouse*, *Passenger*, *Hole* (a host object), *Boundary*, *Shadow*. Note that *Hole* is both `+D` and rigid — dependence is not confined to roles.

**`−D`** — an instance could exist in a world containing nothing else of relevance: *Person* (organs are parts), *Rock*, *Physical Object*, *Statue* (clay is a constituent), *Team* (members are members, though *Team Member* is `+D`).

**Test:** imagine one instance alone in an otherwise empty universe. A lone *rock* is coherent → `−D`. A lone *student*, with no institution and nothing to study, is not → `+D`.

Record the dependee rather than a bare label: `+D (on Educational Institution)`.
